# Contradiction Search

## Purpose

After forming a preliminary view, attempt to disprove or materially narrow it. This is required for the main conclusion and for claims that are central, surprising, strategic, causal, disputed, normative, or superlative.

## Generate competing hypotheses

Rewrite the preliminary claim into plausible alternatives:

- direct negation;
- opposite timing or choice;
- conditional exception;
- version/population dependence;
- reverse causality;
- measurement or selection-bias explanation;
- “no meaningful difference” null hypothesis.

Example preliminary claim: `Dark Gift should usually be used on Tavern Tier 4.`

Challenge queries:

- `why save Dark Gift until T5`
- `Dark Gift T4 bad`
- `Dark Gift T5 better`
- `don't use Dark Gift T4`
- `Dark Gift timing disagreement`

Use current patch/season markers and the relevant mode.

## Evaluate counterevidence fairly

For each serious competing claim:

1. identify its strongest supporting evidence, not the weakest formulation;
2. check source authority, independence, freshness, and scope using the same standards;
3. determine whether the conflict is direct or condition-dependent;
4. test whether one view explains more evidence with fewer exceptions;
5. update or split the original claim.

Do not manufacture false balance. A weak isolated objection does not equal strong primary evidence, but it may reveal an exception.

## Outcomes

- `survives`: material counterevidence was sought and did not change the claim;
- `narrowed`: the claim holds only under stated conditions;
- `contested`: credible evidence supports competing views;
- `reversed`: counterevidence is stronger than the preliminary conclusion;
- `unresolved`: available evidence cannot decide.

Use the [contradiction report template](templates/contradiction-report.md) and propagate the outcome into the evidence matrix, confidence, exceptions, and unknowns.
