# Verification

## Claim-level checks

For every consequential claim, answer:

1. Does the inspected source actually say or demonstrate this?
2. Is the source competent and proximal for this claim type?
3. Does its scope match the claim's population, mode, geography, time, and version?
4. Is the evidence direct, or is an inference required?
5. Does corroboration have an independent origin?
6. What evidence would make the claim false or narrower?
7. Is the wording calibrated to remaining uncertainty?

Failure on semantic support is fatal for that citation; no authority score can fix it.

## Burden by claim type

### Official fact

One high-quality primary source may be sufficient if it directly establishes the narrow fact and is current or version-compatible.

### Statistical claim

Check sample size, timeframe, version/patch, rank/MMR or population, filters, metric definition, denominator, missing observations, and possible selection bias. Distinguish descriptive correlation from causal explanation.

### Strategy claim

Seek at least two independent confirmations when available. Strong combinations are:

- relevant statistics plus a demonstrated expert who explains conditions;
- multiple independent experts plus bounded cross-platform community consensus.

If only anecdotes exist, report a hypothesis or practice pattern, not a validated rule.

### Strong superlative

Define the comparison set and metric. Use “best,” “always,” “never,” or “the community agrees” only with unusually strong and representative evidence. Otherwise prefer “among the strongest,” “often,” “under these conditions,” or “among the reviewed sources.”

## Independence check

Trace evidence backward to its origin. Mark shared `lineage_id` for sources using the same dataset, announcement, post, test, datamine, or upstream article. Separate independent measurement from independent interpretation.

## Conflict resolution

When evidence conflicts, test in this order:

1. different definitions or metrics;
2. different date, patch, season, or version;
3. different population, rank, geography, or mode;
4. sample and filter differences;
5. authority and proximity;
6. data-quality or methodological weaknesses;
7. genuine unresolved disagreement.

Do not average incompatible claims into false consensus. Narrow the conclusion or preserve the conflict.

## Failed access

If a source cannot be inspected, do not infer its content from a title or quotation elsewhere. Seek original mirrors, archives, or independent confirmation. If X, Reddit, YouTube, forums, a paywalled source, or a dataset cannot be adequately checked, list that channel as a coverage limitation.
