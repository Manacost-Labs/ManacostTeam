# Gaming Research Example

This is a process example, not a factual answer. It deliberately contains no invented sources or conclusions.

## Request

```text
research: deep, current-patch-only
When should the first Dark Gift be used in Hearthstone Battlegrounds?
```

## Routing

- Type: strategic + statistical + community intelligence.
- Domain: gaming + Hearthstone.
- Context required before evidence: current Battlegrounds patch, season, Dark Gift rules, MMR/rank scope, and what “should” optimizes—survival, average placement, top-four rate, first-place rate, or another outcome.
- Conditional modules: freshness, community intelligence, contradiction search, confidence.

## Recursive research tree

```text
First Dark Gift timing
├── Current mechanic
│   ├── first charge timing
│   ├── pool-generation rule
│   ├── Tavern Tier dependency
│   └── patch/season changes
├── Candidate choices
│   ├── use immediately
│   ├── use on intermediate tier
│   └── hold for later tier
├── Outcomes
│   ├── immediate tempo and survival
│   ├── economy and leveling cost
│   ├── expected future value
│   └── placement metrics
├── Conditional factors
│   ├── hero and armor/health state
│   ├── tribe/minion-type direction
│   ├── lobby strength and MMR
│   └── active season systems
└── Challenge
    ├── cases where common timing fails
    ├── expert disagreements
    └── statistical/community mismatch
```

## Search passes

1. Discover official terminology, current patch notes, data providers, current experts, and community venues with ChatGPT Search/Web.
2. Open official mechanics and current statistical methodology/data; inspect expert analyses and relevant discussion context.
3. Search gaps such as missing pool rules, unknown filters, and under-covered conditional branches.
4. Challenge the leading timing with opposite-tier and “use now versus hold” queries.
5. Verify every source's current-patch compatibility.
6. Audit each strategic claim against the evidence matrix.

## Required evidence logic

- Mechanic claim: current official evidence, or official text plus reproducible testing if the rule is not documented.
- Performance claim: current sample, metric, MMR/rank, timeframe, filters, and patch.
- Strategy claim: normally statistics plus qualified current high-MMR reasoning, with community evidence used for conditions and recurring mistakes.
- Final recommendation: conditional rather than universal unless evidence compares all important alternatives.

## Expected gaps

- Public data may not expose the precise decision point or unchosen alternatives.
- End-state stats may have survivorship bias.
- Expert videos may describe a previous patch.
- Community discussion may overrepresent unusual games.

Those gaps must reduce confidence or narrow the recommendation; they must not be filled by intuition.
